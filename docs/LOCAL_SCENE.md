# Local scene, DPv2 spec sheet (Level 1 candidate, 2026-11-05)

Status: Level 1 signed off 2026-11-05 for the pin-circle measure and again for the hybrid measure (section 16, towns on the boundary). Not in the live catalog, not shown in the app. No "validated" claim is allowed until Level 2 passes (section 8). Display decisions (a switch, labels on the list, a place page section, the cut-off) are open and not covered here; this sheet is about the measure. Supersedes the earlier audit of the old formula and the independent-share option, which did not separate the owner's places (docs/VIBE_VALIDATION.md).

## 1. Job and construct
The tag answers "does this place have a lively scene of places to linger": a compact mix of independent galleries, bookstores and record stores, cafes and bars. It describes character, not quality, and is not a pillar and not in any total. It started from Larchmont having something Pelham lacks. Today's live version (old formula on business names) stays untouched: Aura (half of it), the twin finder and vibe matching still read `local_scene_score`.

## 2. Evidence tier
Construct: weak. Published support is for the ingredients, not for "vibe": third places and community cohesion (Jeffres 2009 national survey, a Los Angeles study), urban vitality from density and mix of points of interest (Jacobs and later GIS studies), the creative-class literature (contested by Glaeser). No validated "cool or vibe" index for small US places exists. Published "coolest places" lists (Time Out, APA, travel blogs) are not used to define or tune the measure (owner decision: editorial criteria such as diversity and "right now" energy); they were a sanity check only (median 89th percentile on cultural venues for 17 Time Out US picks in the catalog).
Ground truth used: the owner's own judgments, 28 places that have it, 12 that do not, 7 in between (docs/VIBE_VALIDATION.md). One judge, survey-level evidence.

## 3. Inputs
Named OpenStreetMap venues of seven types (cafe, bar, restaurant, gallery, bookstore, records, theater) within each place's amenities radius (1,200 m when its stored list reached at most 1,210 m, else 1,500 m), from a re-read of the private Overpass server on 2026-11-05: 1,476 places, 0 failures, 107,207 venues, 8,184 with an OSM brand or brand:wikidata tag (`scripts/catalog/reread_brand_tags.py`; the code now keeps the two tags in stored business lists). Density: activity per acre (people plus jobs) from the Active Outdoors recompute, the continuous factor DPv2 uses instead of the area-type label (principle 14).

## 4. Definition
Per place: culture venues = independent galleries, bookstores and record stores (no brand tag; theaters excluded); culture share = culture venues as a percent of all named venues; hangout share = cafes, bars, galleries, bookstores, record stores and theaters as a percent of all named venues; hangouts in 5 minutes = cafes and bars within 400 m. Reading = each share ranked among places in the same density band (activity per acre, five equal bands), the two ranks averaged. Lively = top quarter of its band and at least 15 venues. Code: `pillars/local_scene_v2.py`, `scripts/catalog/add_local_scene_v2_offline.py`, 9 unit tests. Stored additively as `score.local_scene_v2`; nothing else in the catalog files changes.

## 11. Level 2 check 1, pin-shift (run 2026-11-05, offline, criteria as fixed in section 8)
Method: baseline and shifted facts both use venues within 1,000 m of the pin (the stored reads cover 1,500 m, so a 500 m shift is fully covered); each shifted share is ranked against the baseline distribution of its density band; 1,476 places, 5,003 shifts (north, south, east, west at 500 m).
Result: median absolute change in the reading 3.6 points (limit 5, pass); 95th percentile 28.2 points (limit 20, FAIL); lively unchanged for 92.6 percent of all shifts (limit 80 percent, pass). The 92.6 percent is flattered by the many places that are never lively; among the 213 places lively at 1,000 m only 75.6 percent of shifts keep them lively (207 of 849 shifts lose it, 161 non-lively shifts gain it). Check 1 as fixed: FAIL on the 95th percentile.
Examples (reading at 1,000 m, largest change over four shifts): Larchmont 66.9 lively (5.1), Bronxville 68.5 lively (2.9), Carroll Gardens 75.8 lively (19.9), Park Slope 76.7 lively (11.9), Tribeca 71.3 not lively (9.7), West Village 57.4 not lively (19.7), Murray Hill 37.6 (9.4), Pelham 45.1 (0.0). Note that at the 1,000 m radius Larchmont and Bronxville are lively and Tribeca and the West Village are not, so the radius changes who is lively, as the sensitivity table showed.
Reading: the measure moves by a few points on average but by more than 20 for about one place in twenty when the pin moves 500 m, the same pin sensitivity found for the old tract-level measures. Level 2 cannot pass as specified. The usual remedy here is to pool the venues around the pin and four points 500 m away (as the housing and status label pools do); not designed or tested yet.

## 12. Pooled version, criteria fixed before running (2026-11-05, owner approved trying it)
Definition tested: pool the named venues within 600 m of the pin or of any of four points 400 m north, east, south and west of it (a cross shape reaching 1,000 m; it is sized so that a pin moved 500 m still falls inside the stored 1,500 m reads). Shares, density bands, the reading and "lively" are computed exactly as in section 4 on the pooled venues. Hangouts in 5 minutes stays the count within 400 m of the pin.
Pass criteria, all four required: (1) median absolute change in the reading under a 500 m pin shift at most 5 points; (2) 95th percentile at most 20; (3) lively unchanged for at least 80 percent of all shifts, and the share of baseline-lively places that stay lively reported (target at least 85 percent, judgment); (4) no regress in ordering: at least 92 percent of the owner's (has it, does not) pairs ranked correctly (the as-built version scored 97 percent). Limits are judgments, as in the status signal checks. If it fails, the failure is recorded and the as-built version stays.

### Pooled version result (run 2026-11-05)
Criteria as fixed above, 1,476 places, 4,915 shifts: (1) median change 3.9 points, pass; (2) 95th percentile 30.6, FAIL (the as-built version scored 28.2); (3) lively unchanged for 92.1 percent of all shifts, pass, but only 72.9 percent of baseline-lively places stay lively (target 85 percent), not met; (4) ordering on the owner's labels 91 percent of 336 pairs, below the 92 percent floor, FAIL. Diagnosis: the instability is mostly small-sample noise, not pin geometry. Options (not run): (a) leave as built, record Level 2 check 1 as failed, show the facts with their sample size and never present a rank or flag as stable for places with under 100 venues; (b) shrink each share toward its density-band average by pseudo-counts before ranking (a standard small-sample fix, adds one unsourced parameter, needs new criteria and a retest); (c) raise the minimum venues to 100, which stabilizes the reading but leaves about 7 percent of places eligible.

## 13. Shrinkage version, criteria fixed before running (2026-11-05, owner approved trying it)
Definition tested: before ranking, each share is pulled toward the venue-weighted share of its density band by k pseudo-venues: shrunk share = 100 x (count + k x band share) / (venues + k), for the culture share and the hangout share. Primary k = 30 (a place with 30 venues weighs its own data and the band average equally; a judgment, unsourced); sensitivity k = 10 and 60. Everything else as built (five density bands, reading = mean of the two within-band ranks, lively = top quarter of its band with at least 15 venues), evaluated at the 1,000 m radius used for the pin-shift check so a 500 m shift stays inside the stored 1,500 m reads.
Pass criteria, all four required (same limits as the pooled test): (1) median absolute change in the reading under a 500 m pin shift at most 5 points; (2) 95th percentile at most 20; (3) lively unchanged for at least 80 percent of all shifts and at least 85 percent of baseline-lively places staying lively; (4) at least 92 percent of the owner's 336 (has it, does not) pairs ranked correctly. If k = 30 fails, the failure is recorded and the as-built version stays; k is not tuned to pass.

## 14. Downtown-core version, criteria fixed before running (2026-11-05, owner: the question is whether the downtown has a scene, not what surrounds the pin)
Definition tested: find the place's core and measure the scene there. Core = the hangout venue (cafe, bar, gallery, bookstore, record store, theater) within 600 m of the pin that has the most hangout venues within 300 m of it (ties go to the one nearest the pin). Facts, shares, density bands, the reading and "lively" are then computed exactly as in section 4 on the named venues within 400 m of the core. The search reach is sized to the stored 1,500 m reads: a core up to 600 m from a pin that is itself shifted 500 m keeps all its venues in range. Limit: a town whose downtown is more than 600 m from the catalog pin is not found by this test (Rye and Hastings-on-Hudson are the check cases).
The pin-shift check becomes a check that the same core and reading are found when the pin moves. Pass criteria, all four required (limits unchanged from sections 12 and 13): (1) median absolute change in the reading under a 500 m pin shift at most 5 points; (2) 95th percentile at most 20; (3) lively unchanged for at least 80 percent of all shifts and at least 85 percent of baseline-lively places staying lively; (4) at least 92 percent of the owner's 336 (has it, does not) pairs ranked correctly. Reported only: Larchmont, Bronxville, Pelham, Rye, Hastings-on-Hudson, Tribeca, Murray Hill, and how far each core is from its pin. If it fails, the failure is recorded.

## 15. Boundary version, criteria fixed before running (2026-11-05, owner approved)
Definition tested: measure the place, not a circle: every named venue of the seven types inside the boundary of the Census geography the catalog place was measured on (the same place or county subdivision geoid as the diversity read), so a town's scene is its whole mapped town, with no pin involved. Facts, shares, density bands (activity per acre of the catalog pin), the reading and "lively" as in section 4, on the venues inside the boundary. Scope: the 885 catalog places measured at place or county subdivision level; the 580 tract-level neighborhoods are not tested here (their boundary is one tract, which the pin still selects). Places whose boundary is wider than a 3 km search circle around its centroid are excluded and counted.
Data: Census 2022 cartographic boundary files (cb_2022_<state>_place_500k and cb_2022_<state>_cousub_500k for the 18 catalog states) downloaded once into their own folder outside the repo; venues read from the private Overpass server around each boundary's centroid and kept only when the point lies inside the boundary.
Pass criteria: (1) stability: at least 90 percent of 500 m pin shifts (north, south, east, west) stay inside the same boundary, since the measure changes only when the pin crosses into another town; (2) ordering: among the owner's labeled places that are in scope, at least 85 percent of (has it, does not) pairs ranked correctly, run only if there are at least 3 places of each kind, otherwise reported as not testable; (3) coverage reported: places with a boundary, places with at least 15 venues, lively count, and the owner's named towns (Larchmont, Bronxville, Pelham, Rye, Hastings-on-Hudson, Mamaroneck, Cambridge, Santa Monica) with venues, reading and lively. ## Decision 2026-11-06: the visible tag is activity, not the culture and hangout mix
Owner direction: the tag answers "is this place quiet and boring, does it have some vibrancy, or is it vibrant as hell", not "is it cool". The mix measure (sections 1 to 16) could not separate busy from ordinary in dense neighborhoods and missed places the owner calls has-it (Silver Lake, Los Feliz, Venice); the wider venue sets (G, G2 in docs/VIBE_VALIDATION.md) did not fix it. The tag is now independent named venues (cafes, bars, restaurants, galleries, bookstores, record stores, theaters, no OSM brand tag) within 1,200 m of the pin, for every place: Quiet under 13 (about half the catalog), Some 13 to 70, Vibrant 71 or more (about 15%). Cuts are the catalog median and 85th percentile on 2026-11-06 (unsourced, not validated). Known limits: counts reward long commercial strips as much as compact centers (Sunnyside, Bay Ridge read Vibrant while Bronxville and Silver Lake read Some, against the owner's sense of busyness); places with few mapped venues read Quiet (Rye, 7); no judgment about the kind of business is made or allowed (the owner's ruling of 2026-10-28 on race and income also rules out ethnicity as a proxy). The mix facts stay stored in score.local_scene_v2 and are not shown. Aura still reads the old score and is to be rebuilt separately.

## Decision 2026-11-06: Aura is an and-rule (pushed ba67ab88)
Owner direction: Aura could not stay on the old local scene score. The weighted version (50% scene, 35% livability, 15% status, top 5% per metro) was tried on the new measures and saturated: venue count rank was 95 to 100 for nearly every dense neighborhood, so wealth and livability decided it, and Battery Park City, the Financial District and Brooklyn Heights ranked in the top ten of New York against the owner's view. A mix-based weighted version put Brooklyn neighborhoods above Tribeca and SoHo and still let in leafy suburbs. Aura is now true only when all of: independent venues within 1,200 m at least 71 (Vibrant), mix reading (local_scene_v2.combined) at least 65 with at least 15 venues, status class Affluent or Elite, and not a job center (more than 5 workers per resident where LODES data exists). No weights, no quota. ## Decision 2026-11-10 (owner): the activity tag is a data measure of how busy a place is; Level 1 is final
Decision: the tag stays a count of independent named venues within 1,200 m (Quiet under 13, Some 13 to 70, Vibrant 71 or more). It is not adjusted to match the owner's own sense of which places have a scene. Where the count and the owner's judgment disagree (Murray Hill, Battery Park City, Sunnyside, Bay Ridge read Vibrant; Silver Lake, Venice, Larchmont, Bronxville read Some), the count stands, because the tag cannot see who is in a place and the 2026-10-28 ruling forbids using race or income as a stand-in.
Why: the independent rater check (2c) failed for both raters returned (Spearman about 0.34 against 0.70; 70 to 72 percent of pairs ranked correctly against 80), the raters agreed with each other only partly (0.641), and every redesign tried (pooled, shrinkage, downtown core, boundary, wider venue sets) either failed or did not separate busy from ordinary. No other measurable definition of "has a scene" is on the table.
Wording rule: copy describes the tag as how busy a place is (venue activity). It never says "cool", "has a scene", or "validated". Places with few mapped venues are shown with their venue count, because a thinly mapped town reads Quiet (gap A3).
Level: Level 1 (Specified) is final for the activity tag. Level 2 is not pursued. Accepted failures: pin-shift of the Aura rule (75.0 percent retention against 80), launch pairs (3 of 6 against 5), labels 2c (both raters) and 2d (split places). The Aura and-rule is not re-decided here; it stays at Level 1 with those failures accepted unless the owner removes it.
