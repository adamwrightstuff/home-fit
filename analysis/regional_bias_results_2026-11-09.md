Region A multiplier on the stored count at equal USGS named trails: 3.97 (95% interval 2.75 to 5.74); hiker-flagged 3.83; USGS miles 3.98; dropping IL, GA and AZ (n=26) 4.16. Pass rule (at most 1.5) FAILS. USGS slope on the stored count is only 0.08 to 0.15, so USGS barely tracks the stored count at all.
Reading: USGS shows no regional difference in trails while the stored count shows 4x, which under the pre-written reading points to OSM mapping or the destination definition, not a real trail difference. That reading is weak: USGS coverage is uneven by state, USGS tracks the stored count poorly even within a region, n is 40, and the stored count is destinations (routes plus parks and protected land of at least 100 ha) while USGS counts trail segments by name. This check cannot separate mapping density from the destination definition.

## PAD-US correction and access-filter variants (2026-11-10)
Re-download of the 18 PAD-US files (MD5 matched), deleted after. Per-place counts for three access rules: analysis/regional_bias_padus_variants_2026-11-10.csv.
Flaw in my first PAD-US step: I kept only Open and Restricted access (OA, RA). PAD-US access is unknown (UK) or closed (XA) for 62% of units of at least 100 ha in the Northeast and Chicago states against 38% elsewhere (CT 93%, DE 92%, MD 89%, PA 77%, NC 64%, against NV 11%, WA 21%, AZ 22%). Dropping them undercounted the Northeast, so the first PAD-US regional gap (0.63 SD) was too small and the overstatement of our OSM area count (0.50 SD) too large.
Corrected, OSM area count gap 1.13 SD against PAD-US: OA+RA 0.63 (overstatement 0.50); OA+RA+UK 0.72 (0.41); all access 0.82 (0.31). Spearman of PAD-US count with the OSM area count: 0.614, 0.626, 0.667. Reading: most of the parks and protected land excess is real; the overstatement is between 0.3 and 0.5 SD depending on whether unknown-access and closed land counts as hikeable, and the remaining gap is not an artifact the check can pin down. The strongest unconfirmed part is routes.
Option B on both terms (PAD-US replaces the OSM count at 15 km and 5 km, refit medians) is much more disruptive than the 15 km-only estimate: 851 to 887 places move more than 5 active outdoors points and 352 to 361 more than 10; Connecticut places fall 16 to 23 points on average (PAD-US count 9 to 21 against a stored 103). AO total gap 20.3 to 11.9 to 13.1.
Connecticut inspection against the PAD-US data: Sherwood Island State Park is 274 acres in PAD-US (counted). Devil's Den/Saugatuck Forest is about 1,800 acres in PAD-US but access is closed or unknown, so it was filtered out; Trout Brook Valley is 304 acres, unknown access; Brett Woods 41 acres; Lake Mohegan Recreation Area 113 acres. Ward Pound Ridge (NY) is not within 15 km of Wilton. Fragmentation of land trust parcels (many under 100 ha) is a second reason CT counts are low, as raised by the Muse agent; both reasons mean a unit count with a size cutoff and an access filter does not describe connected hiking networks.
